Unseated

The tower took a hit. The graphics card ripped out of its slot and landed fans-up on the PSU shroud, and the impact tore its middle fan section clean off. What's left is a three-lane map on three levels: floor lanes either side of the card, a crater of exposed circuit board in the middle, and two surviving fan decks up high at each end.

Three levels

The deck sits 14.3 m up, the height of Crash's tallest rooftop. The board is a 1.8 m step above the floor, so players can mantle between them anywhere along the crater's open sides. Getting onto the deck takes a ramp, and there are only four of them.

Floor0 m

The PSU shroud. Two long lanes run past the card: a cluttered cable alley against the motherboard and a wider lane along the shattered side glass.

Board1.8 m

The exposed circuit board where the middle fan used to be. The GPU die is the raised objective in the centre, ringed by memory chips (knee-high cover) and power chokes (full cover).

Deck14.3 m

The tops of the two surviving fan sections. Each is a ring of walkway around a fan pit. Two bent heatpipes bridge the crater between them at the same height.

Off-limits

  • The fin stacks. They fill both fan sections under the decks. Their torn faces form the crater's end walls, 12.5 m of exposed fins. At this scale the fins are 0.35–0.7 m apart, narrower than a 0.76 m player, so render them as walls and block them with collision.
  • The fan pits. Each is a hole about 32 m across in the middle of a deck. The blades sheared off, so the pit drops straight into the fins. Make it a kill volume, or net it with debris so nobody falls in.
  • The fan hubs. They sit in the middle of each pit, visible but out of reach. You could add a risky jump route later.
  • Under the shroud. The power supply's area stays sealed. Sparks from the cable holes give the floor some light and noise. Power-supply capacitors can hold charge after the power goes out, which explains the sparks.
  • Outside the case. The map ends at the glass rubble on one side and the motherboard on the other.

Damage that does a job

  • Bent heatpipes. Two curl up from the board to the decks as ramps, and two still span the crater as bridges.
  • Torn shroud flaps stand up at the crater edge of each deck. They break the deck-to-deck sniper line.
  • A shroud lip overhangs both floor lanes by 1–2 m, so a player on the deck rim can't see the half of the lane nearest the card.
  • The melted power connector on the front deck is Team B's landmark. Its cable runs down to their spawn as their ramp up.
  • Dust bunnies are 2–4 m clumps. Use them as soft cover that blocks sight but not bullets.
  • RGB LEDs on standby power still flicker, which gives you light sources in a dead machine.
  • Safe drops. Add one or two cable piles under each deck. A 14 m fall should kill, so these are the only quick ways down.

Assumptions

  • Scale is 357×, so the card's 304 mm length matches Crash's 109 m long side. The player is CoD4's 70 × 30-unit hull, with 1 unit = 1 inch.
  • The card is a made-up three-fan design built from typical part sizes. It is not any specific product. The gaps between card, motherboard and side panel are set for gameplay.
  • The layout is rotationally symmetric: turn it 180° and Team A's side matches Team B's. The two floor lanes are deliberately different, one close range and one mid range.
  • For Domination, put B on the die and A and C on each team's end deck.
